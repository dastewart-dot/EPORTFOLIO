MY STORY

Leadership became part of my creative voice.

I entered producing with a background shaped by military service, business, psychology, travel, and a growing commitment to storytelling. NYFA helped me connect those experiences to a professional producing process. I learned to think about the audience, budget, crew, schedule, legal responsibilities, pitch, visual identity, and the path from concept to completed work.

This portfolio is intentionally built around projects that show different parts of that growth: creative development, television world-building, production collaboration, research, business creation, and reflection.

8 EXPANDED REFLECTIONS

How NYFA changed the way I think as a producer.

1. Finding My Producer Voice

I learned that the producer’s voice is expressed through decisions: what story to protect, what resources to prioritize, and how to move a project forward. I began to see producing as creative leadership rather than simply making things happen.

2. Collaboration and Communication

Collaboration taught me that even a strong idea can fail if communication is unclear. I now value preparation and communication as creative tools that build trust and reduce confusion.

3. From Idea to Package

One Way Route taught me to think about story, visual identity, budgeting, scheduling, casting, and audience together. I learned what makes a concept feel producible.

4. Building a Television World

Full Circle and 30 Seconds Away taught me to think about character growth, episode engines, season escalation, audience hooks, and long-term storytelling potential.

5. Research as a Producing Tool

Mixed methods research showed me how data and human experience can work together to strengthen audience research, comparable analysis, and creative decisions.

6. Business and the Creative Process

Entrepreneurship, business affairs, finance, and law helped me understand that creative ideas need contracts, budgets, markets, financing strategies, and distribution paths.

7. Curation and Presentation

Curation changed how I think about what to show, what to leave out, and how sequence and visual tone shape audience perception. That lesson directly shaped this revised portfolio.

8. What Comes Next

My next step is to keep turning class projects into living projects beyond the classroom and continue building Stew Creative Productions.
