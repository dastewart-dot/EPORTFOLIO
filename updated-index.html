ABOUT THE PRODUCER

Creative leadership shaped by experience.

I am a producer, writer, creator and actor whose approach to filmmaking is shaped by leadership, discipline, business, psychology, aviation and hands-on entertainment experience. I am interested in character-driven film and television projects that explore family, identity, ambition, resilience and second chances. As a producer, I want a strong creative idea to be supported by an equally strong plan for development, people, resources, scheduling and execution.

My professional background has taught me to work in environments where preparation, accountability and communication matter. Twelve years of military service beginning in 2014 strengthened my leadership, adaptability and ability to remain focused under pressure. Seven years in the aviation industry beginning in 2019 reinforced precision, safety, teamwork and attention to detail. I bring those qualities into production by thinking ahead, identifying problems early and understanding that every department contributes to the final project.

My entertainment experience gives me perspectives from several sides of the industry. I participated in a producer internship program at ESPN from 2018–2019, worked as a producer assistant with NFL Network from 2019–2020, and served as an assistant producer for the Joe Budden Network from 2020–2021. Since 2020, I have also worked as a background actor/extra on ten film productions. Acting has made me more attentive to performance, character motivation, communication with talent and the atmosphere a production creates for actors.

My graduate work at New York Film Academy strengthened that professional foundation through development, line producing, production accounting, pitching, research, entertainment law, marketing, finance, collaboration, television development and entrepreneurship. I earned my Master's in Producing and Innovation in 2026 after earning an Associate degree in Business Administration from Hudson Valley Community College in 2012 and a Bachelor's degree in Psychology from the University at Buffalo in 2014.

REFLECTIONS

How my producing practice developed.

1. Finding My Producer Voice

At the beginning of the program, I thought of producing mainly as the work required to organize a project and get it made. Through development, pitching and production exercises, I learned that a producer also has a creative voice. That voice appears in the projects I choose, the questions I ask, the resources I prioritize and the way I communicate a vision to other people.

I now look beyond whether an idea interests me and ask whether I understand its audience, scope, production needs and long-term potential. I have become more intentional about balancing the creative side of a project with the practical decisions necessary to move it forward.

2. Collaboration and Communication

Production work reinforced that a good idea cannot carry a project by itself. Every department needs information, expectations and a shared understanding of what the production is trying to accomplish.

That lesson has made me a stronger producer because I now see communication as part of the creative process rather than an administrative task.

3. From Idea to Production Package

Developing One Way Route pushed me to think beyond the concept itself. The pitch, storyboards and planning materials forced me to examine how the story would actually be produced.

I learned that a strong package creates confidence and demonstrates that the producer understands both the story and the work required to execute it.

4. Building a Television World

Full Circle taught me that television development requires a different kind of planning from a single short film. Characters need enough depth to sustain multiple episodes, conflicts need room to grow and the world must continually generate new story possibilities.

Working on the series bible and pilot helped me understand how character, theme and episode structure support one another.

5. Research as a Producing Tool

Research Methodologies expanded my understanding of how evidence can support producing decisions. Quantitative research can reveal patterns while qualitative research can provide context.

As a producer, I can use both approaches when researching audiences, comparable projects, trends or the real-world subjects behind a story.

6. Business Behind the Creative Work

Courses involving finance, law, marketing and entrepreneurship showed me that producing continues far beyond development and the set.

Building Stew Creative Productions helped connect those lessons and gave me a more complete view of producing as both a creative profession and a business.

7. Curation and Professional Presentation

Curation taught me that presentation is itself a form of communication. A portfolio should guide the viewer toward work that demonstrates specific skills, development and professional direction.

Revising this portfolio made that lesson practical by focusing each artifact on the project, assignment, course and skills demonstrated.

8. Moving Forward

The program gave me a broader understanding of the producer's responsibility from idea generation through development, production, business planning and presentation.

My next step is to keep developing original work, strengthen Stew Creative Productions and continue building relationships with collaborators who can help move projects toward production and distribution.
